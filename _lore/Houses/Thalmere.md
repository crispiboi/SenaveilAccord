Patient and quietly ruthless.

## In the Council

- **How it plays politics:** Rarely bid openly, yet end up holding things.
- **Why it keeps the peace:** Real but transactional: they remember exactly what you owe.
- **At the founding:** Paid for the rebuilding of Senaveil and quietly wrote the procedural rules.

## On the Ledger

- **Starting office:** Yards
- **Starting Standing:** 30 marks
- **Starting favours and grievances:** Holds a favour from [[Varenne]] for the rebuilding of Senaveil.

## Worlds

A trailblazer house. It paid to settle [[Alba]] and holds claims there, as well as living on [[Tellane]]. The moon [[Garrick]] carries a founder's name from this house.

## Customs

- **House honour:** The Keel, for building something that lasts.
- **Names:** Plain, solid Anglo, with trade and craft surnames: Garrick, Rowena, Ashby, Hartsell.
- **Ship names:** Works: foundries, bridges and yards on large ships, tools on small ones.

## Voice

**The account.** Where Ashgrove asks whether a thing is right, Thalmere asks whether it adds up. Every question is an equation to solve and a deal to make. It is the most precise house in the Council, with exact figures, exact terms and exact debts, and because it has done the sums, it is the house that hedges its bets and then, when the numbers say the moment has come, is first to take the risk.

**How it speaks:** short, plain and exact. It uses figures, dates, tonnages and odds, never "about" or "many". It talks in terms of trade: cost, terms, return, owed. It speaks late, briefly and without heat, and its humour is dry enough to miss.

**In Council:** it reframes every debate as a balance sheet. It agrees, with conditions, and the conditions are where its interests live. When a gamble is worth it, it puts in the first stake and makes sure everyone remembers who did.

**First question in a crisis:** does it add up?

> "We have no objection. We have a condition."
>
> "The odds are poor. They will not get better. We'll go first."
